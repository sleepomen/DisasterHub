# Simulation and routing

[← README](../README.md) · English · [繁體中文](zh-TW/simulation-and-routing.md)

## Simulation State and Occupancy

- The simulation flow: `/api/simulate_disaster` uses PostGIS to find the affected shelters and estimates the population inside the radius, the evacuation demand and the shelter shortfall from the township populations in `data_reference/east_taiwan_population.json`. It then computes the evacuation plan (see [Evacuation Assignment](#evacuation-assignment)) and writes the resulting occupancy to the database before responding, recomputing vector documents only for the shelters that changed. From then on the loads the AI sees match the map, whether the answer comes from RAG, capacity ranking, geographic distance, or the simulation snapshot. Questions about the simulation are answered by code from the snapshot (see [Simulation follow-ups](#simulation-follow-ups)); the model never sees the simulation. When a summary used to be attached to ordinary questions, llama3.2:3b mixed the two, answering 「我們一家五口可以去哪」 with a simulated shelter's name next to another shelter's township and capacity.
- The data sync at startup and via `/api/sync` only updates capacity, address and coordinates — it does **not** reset occupancy (it is only clamped down when the capacity shrinks below the current headcount). To clear occupancy, use "Clear all layers" in the UI or call `/api/reset_simulation`.
- The sync also **deletes** shelters that are no longer in the source JSON. Without it, removing a shelter from the source left its row in the database forever and the AI kept recommending a place that is no longer a shelter; a rename left both the old and the new name. Because this is the only step that deletes data, it runs only when the sync is complete and trustworthy — every source row was written, no source file failed to parse, and no individual row was skipped. One source file is one county, so a single unparsable file would otherwise wipe that whole county. When any of those does not hold, the deletion is skipped, a warning says why, and `sync.prune_skipped` in `/api/stats` counts it; a counter that keeps rising means the source data is broken and nobody noticed.
- After occupancy is written back, the snapshot's "current remaining space" is recomputed from the per-shelter figures, while "placeable / shortfall" stay as the planning numbers from the moment of simulation and the number of people already placed is reported separately, so the AI's totals never contradict its per-shelter list.
- Simulation, write-back and reset all mutate the database or global state, and once deployed anyone who can reach the domain can call these endpoints directly, so they require a login. Operators log in once when they open the site; the session cookie is sent automatically by the browser and nothing has to be typed again while it is valid. Without logging in the UI is read-only (map and chat).

## Evacuation Assignment

The evacuation plan is computed on the backend (`services/simulation/evacuation.py`). It used to be done in the browser: crowd points were scattered at random and each one picked a shelter, so the same parameters gave a different allocation every run, and the figures the AI quoted only matched the map after the animation finished and the write-back succeeded.

1. **Origins.** Every affected village (村里) is an origin: 554 villages with their registered population from the Ministry of the Interior (September 2026, 965,887 people; `data_reference/east_villages.json`, built by `tools/data/build_villages.py`). A village's weight decays with its distance from the disaster centre, and the evacuation demand is split across villages in proportion to it, using the largest-remainder method so the parts add up exactly to the reported total. The origin is not the village's geometric centre, which in a large mountain village often sits on an empty slope: it is the OpenStreetMap building with the most other buildings within 500 m (the largest settlement), chosen among buildings within 1 km of a road. All 554 origins are within 336 m of a road (median 19 m). When the radius contains no village (open sea), a single origin is placed at the disaster center. Until 2026-11-07 the origins were 29 township centroids with rounded population figures, which left out 12 of the 41 townships.
2. **Assignment.** Who goes where is a transportation problem, solved with min-cost flow in the database (pgRouting's `pgr_maxFlowMinCost`, `ShelterRepository.assignment_flows`): no shelter gets more people than its free space, as many people as possible are placed, and among those plans the one with the shortest total travel time for everyone is chosen (on foot or by car, see [Travel time](#travel-time-walking-or-driving)). Pairs with no safe road (see [Avoiding hazard zones](#avoiding-hazard-zones-and-live-alerts)) are left out, and the result is reproducible. Until 2026-11-02 the rule was nearest-first (every pair sorted by time and filled in that order, so each group goes to the closest shelter with room, as it would without coordination); with village origins it places the same number of people but takes on average 3% and at most 18% more total time (see [Assignment evaluation](evaluation.md#assignment-evaluation)). The pure-Python solver (`services/simulation/min_cost_flow.py`) gives the same answer in all 159 evaluation scenarios but takes up to 1.5 s, against 0.3 s in the database; it is kept as the fallback when the database call fails and as the reference in tests. Costs are sent as integer tenths of a second: with floating-point costs Boost's potentials drift to -1e-12 and the function refuses the graph as having a negative edge. Road distances for all pairs come from one many-to-many pgRouting query. A full 10 km simulation of 宜蘭市 (145 villages × 130 shelters, 291 flows) takes 0.9 s end to end, and 30 km around 蘇澳鎮 (229 × 220) takes 2.3 s. Origins may snap to a road within 5 km instead of 1 km, which is only a margin now. A pair without a road distance is costed as straight-line × 1.16, the measured median road/straight ratio (4,287 township centroid–shelter pairs within 30 km: median 1.163, p10 1.07, p90 1.55); comparing a raw straight-line distance against road distances would always favour the unrouted pair. The estimate is then converted to time at walking speed or 30 km/h. If routing fails entirely, the whole plan falls back to straight-line distance.
3. **Write-back.** The plan is written to the database immediately (current occupancy plus the assigned people), and only the changed vector documents are recomputed. If the write fails, the simulation is cleared and the endpoint returns 500, so the AI never describes a plan the database does not hold.

The response carries `evacuation` and `occupancy`. `evacuation` has `flows` (each with `distance_km`, `routed`, `road_km`, `walk_min`, `drive_min`, `hazard_m` and the route as an encoded polyline in `path`), `assigned`, `placed`, `unplaced`, `cut_off`, `unreachable`, `distance_basis` = `road` / `mixed` / `straight`, `mode`, `excluded`, `near_hazard`, `undesignated`, `designation`, `route_hazard` and `alerts`. Across 112 sample scenarios on a grid over the east coast, road distance changed the plan in 66; for example 太麻里鄉's evacuees now go to 香蘭國小 (4.39 km by road, 3.08 km straight) before 北里活動中心, which is closer in a straight line (2.16 km) but 5.44 km by road. The frontend only replays `flows` as an animation and applies `occupancy` when it ends. Asked about the plan, the assistant lists every shelter with its headcount, written by code (see [Simulation follow-ups](#simulation-follow-ups)).

**Limitations:** each village starts from one point, its largest settlement, and the population is the registered one (戶籍), not who is actually there at the time. Because the plan minimises the total, a village is occasionally sent past its nearest shelter when that leaves the nearest one to a village whose next option is much farther.

## Simulation follow-ups

Questions asked while a simulation is running are answered by code from the simulation snapshot (`services/simulation/text.py`), not by the model. `chat/intent.simulation_topics` sorts a question into what it asks for, and the answer is assembled from those parts:

| The question asks | Example | The answer |
|---|---|---|
| allocation | 「每間避難所分配了多少人」 | Every shelter in the plan with its headcount, and how the plan was ordered (walking or driving time) |
| unplaced | 「還有多少人沒有被安置」「缺口多大」 | Evacuees, placed and unplaced, and what to do about the gap |
| affected | 「哪些避難所受影響」「還有空間嗎」 | Every affected shelter with capacity, occupancy and free space, marking the ones not used and why |
| excluded | 「有沒有避難所被排除」 | The shelters not used as destinations and why (inside a hazard zone, not designated for this disaster, no safe road); says so plainly when there are none |
| road bottlenecks | 「哪幾段路會塞」「要多久才能清空」 | When driving, the ten roads that take longest to clear, each with cars, capacity per hour, time and the bridges it crosses (at most three named), the people not counted, and that it is a lower bound; on foot, that it was not estimated and how to see it ([road bottlenecks](#road-bottlenecks-when-driving)) |
| anything else | 「有什麼建議」「模擬結果」 | A summary built from the numbers: population, placed and unplaced, the shortfall or where free space remains, the shelters receiving the most people and, when driving, the slowest road |

A question can ask for several at once (「每間分配多少人？有沒有被排除？」). Hazard, exclusion and route notes are appended once at the end, without repeating what the answer already said.

- **Why.** The simulation follow-up evaluation (see [Simulation follow-up evaluation](evaluation.md#simulation-follow-up-evaluation)) found that the model copied names and numbers correctly but cut long lists short (26 of 63 shelters), took 34–108 s per list, and got the "excluded" question wrong in 4 of 5 scenarios: it said 26 shelters were excluded when none were, listed shelters that had received people, and made up reasons. The correct exclusion notes appended by code then contradicted it.
- **Result.** Complete and correct in all 25 questions (20 before road bottlenecks were added), in 0.1 s.
- **The suggestions are code too.** They come from the same numbers the model was given, so free text only added room for error. The cost is that they are fixed in form.

## Debris-Flow Hazard Zones

Source: the Ministry of Agriculture's open dataset "115年度1753條土石流潛勢溪流影響範圍圖" ([data.gov.tw/dataset/176526](https://data.gov.tw/dataset/176526), Open Government Data License v1). `data_reference/debris_flow_east.geojson` keeps the 531 impact zones in Yilan, Hualien and Taitung, with stream number, township, village, risk level and household count.

- **Rebuilding the file.** `python tools/data/build_hazard_zones.py <path to .shp without extension>` reads the SHP/DBF directly (no GDAL or pyshp needed) and converts TWD97 TM2 (EPSG:3826) to WGS84 with the inverse transverse Mercator formula. Checked against PostGIS `ST_Transform`, the results agree to 1e-8 degrees. Keep the downloaded archive in its own folder outside the project.
- **Import.** The startup sync loads the GeoJSON into the `hazard_zones` table in one transaction (delete, then insert), repairing invalid polygons with `ST_MakeValid`. A failed import is logged and counted as `sync.hazard_failed`, but never fails the shelter sync.
- **Flood simulation.** Heavy rain triggers both flooding and debris flows, so a flood simulation does not send evacuees to shelters inside a debris-flow zone. In practice: 澳花國小 lies inside the high-risk zone 宜縣DF139. Excluded shelters are listed in the response, and the AI answer ends with a fixed sentence naming them, appended by code rather than left to the model. If the hazard query fails, the simulation runs without exclusions instead of failing. Earthquake and fire have no matching hazard data yet, so nothing is excluded for them.
- **Near a zone: warn, do not exclude.** A shelter outside a zone but within 200 m of one (`HAZARD_WARN_M`) is still assigned evacuees; the response lists it under `near_hazard` with the zone numbers and distance, and the same code-appended sentence tells the reader to stay alert during heavy rain. The impact zones already include the run-out area, and excluding near-misses would remove scarce capacity. Measured: only 南山國小 (121 m from 宜縣DF079) falls in that band; the next closest shelter is 482 m away.
- **Nearest shelter.** For AI nearest-shelter questions, a shelter inside a zone is flagged in the context the model receives, with the zone number and a note not to go there during heavy rain; a shelter within 200 m of a zone is flagged with the distance and a note to stay alert. The `/api/nearest_shelter` endpoint itself returns plain distances.
- **Map.** `GET /api/hazard_zones` serves the zones, and the map draws them in light brown, or brick red and amber while a red or yellow alert is in effect; clicking one shows its stream number and risk level.

## Road-Network Distance (pgRouting)

Straight-line distance misleads in eastern Taiwan: mountains and rivers force long detours. From the population centroid of 太麻里鄉, 北里活動中心 is 2.16 km away in a straight line but 5.44 km by road, while 香蘭國小 is 3.08 km straight and only 4.39 km by road. Nearest-shelter answers (`/api/nearest_shelter` and the AI) now rank by road distance.

- **Database image.** `pgrouting/pgrouting:15-3.3-3.4.1` is the same PostgreSQL 15 + PostGIS 3.3 as before, plus pgRouting, so an existing `db_data` volume keeps working: `docker compose up -d` recreates the container on the new image. `ensure_schema` runs `CREATE EXTENSION IF NOT EXISTS pgrouting` in its own transaction; on an image without pgRouting it only logs a warning and distances stay straight-line.
- **Data.** `data_reference/east_roads.json.gz` (2.8 MB) holds the drivable roads of Yilan, Hualien and Taitung from OpenStreetMap: 118,399 edges, 94,640 junctions, 19,397 km, of which 8,130 edges (1,263 km) are one-way. Each edge also keeps its road's name, route number (`ref`, such as 9 for Provincial Highway 9), whether it is a bridge, and its number of lanes, for naming bottlenecks and estimating road capacity. Route numbers cover 93.5% of the length of motorway, primary, secondary and tertiary roads, and 3,100 edges (304 km) are bridges, 61% of them named (富里大橋). Lanes are tagged on only 5% of the length, so a missing count is stored as 0 and capacity has to fall back to a default per road class. Roads are split at junctions, lengths are measured on the full geometry before it is simplified for storage, and fragments under 20 edges are dropped so a location cannot snap onto an isolated parking lane. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0; the file is a derived database and is distributed under the same licence.
- **Rebuilding.** Download `https://download.geofabrik.de/asia/taiwan-latest.osm.pbf` (about 330 MB) into its own folder outside the project, then run `tools/data/build_road_network.py` in a throwaway container (it needs pyosmium; the command is in the script's docstring). It takes about a minute. The county boundaries are stitched from OSM relations; part of the Yilan and Taitung boundaries lies at sea outside the Geofabrik extract, so open chains are joined across those gaps. The first version dropped them and lost both counties' mainland. The straight lines that close Taitung's gaps cut into southern Hualien, so 富里 and 玉里 lie inside both the Hualien and the Taitung ring; with all rings in one even-odd index such a point flips twice and counts as outside, and the second version had no roads in the whole 富里 valley (玉里's centroid was 3.2 km from the "nearest road", 富里鄉公所 3.8 km). Each county now has its own index and a road is kept if it lies in any of them. Since the fix every mainland shelter is within 1 km of a road; the 14 shelters on 綠島 and 蘭嶼 have roads but no road link to the main island, and two 西林 entries in 萬榮鄉 are about 4.4 km from any road (their coordinates are probably off), so these fall back to straight-line distance.
- **Import.** The startup sync loads the file into the `roads` table in one transaction (about 3 seconds) and records its SHA-256 in `dataset_versions`, so unchanged data is skipped on later starts. A failure is logged and counted as `sync.roads_failed` and never fails the shelter sync.
- **Query.** The 10 nearest shelters by straight line (KNN on the index) are re-ranked by travel time. Each point snaps to the nearest road within 1 km, and `pgr_withPointsCost` routes on the edges inside the points' bounding box plus a margin; a pair that finds no path there is retried on the whole network. Road distance = walk to the road + shortest path + walk from the road. A query takes 10–60 ms.
- **Fallback.** Shelters that cannot be routed (point more than 1 km from a road, disconnected network) are listed after the routed ones with their straight-line distance. If routing fails entirely the answer says so and uses straight-line distance; `routing.road` / `routing.fallback` / `routing.failed` in `/api/stats` count each case.
- **pgRouting pitfall.** `pgr_withPointsCost` in 3.4 returns no rows at all when a point's `fraction` is exactly 0 or 1, which happens whenever a location snaps onto a road end; before the fix, a query from Hualien station returned nothing. Fractions are now nudged into (0, 1).
- **Limitations.** Speeds are fixed per road class, with no live traffic and no slope; evacuation congestion is estimated separately for driving and does not change the routes ([road bottlenecks](#road-bottlenecks-when-driving)). Footpaths are not included. Evacuation counters: `routing.evacuation_road` / `routing.evacuation_fallback` / `routing.evacuation_failed`.

## Routes on the map

- **Geometry.** Each flow of the evacuation plan carries its actual route as an encoded polyline (`path`), built in SQL from `pgr_withPoints`. The snapped start and end lie partway along an edge, so every step is cut with `ST_LineSubstring` and reversed when it runs against the edge direction, then the line is simplified to about 10 m.
- **The drawn route is the measured one.** Route length plus the two off-road legs matches the road distance within 0.1–0.3 %; the gap is the 5 m simplification applied when the network file was built.
- **Picking the centre.** Besides the three city presets, clicking anywhere on the map makes that point the simulation centre. `/api/locate` labels it after the nearest village origin within 5 km (「頭城鎮福成里附近」), or as a point at sea; the label is only a name, and near a township border it can name the neighbour. Clicks on a shelter, a hazard zone, a route or an origin open that item instead, and the simulation circle ignores clicks so a new centre can be picked inside it.
- **Origins on the map.** After a simulation every affected village is drawn as a ring at its origin, larger for more people affected, with its name and affected population on hover.
- **Map.** One line per flow, wider for more people. A flow without a route is drawn as a dashed straight line, and a route that still crosses a hazard zone is orange. Evacuees spread about 300 m around their village's origin, walk to the start of the route and follow it. A 10 km simulation in Hualien City routes all 57 flows in 0.07 s.

## Avoiding hazard zones and live alerts

Hazards change the cost of the roads they touch; roads are never dropped silently.

| Condition | Applies to | Effect on roads inside the zone |
|---|---|---|
| Red debris-flow alert | Always (simulations and nearest-shelter answers) | Closed |
| Yellow debris-flow alert | Always | Cost × 5 |
| Debris-flow zone without an alert | Flood simulations | Cost × 3 / 2 / 1.5 for high / medium / low risk; never closed |

- **Which roads are inside.** `road_hazards` records every edge that crosses a zone and the fraction of it inside (`ST_Intersection`). It is rebuilt on every sync, in under a second (2,590 rows). Only the part inside a zone is weighted: mountain edges run for kilometres, and when a whole edge counted, a 南澳 route was reported as 12.87 km inside zones against 0.95 km measured.
- **Why zones are weighted, not closed.** They cover 564 km of road, including the Su-Hua and Hualien–Taitung highways. Closing all of them would cut the east coast apart.
- **Live alerts.** `services/geo/hazard_alerts.py` polls the agency's [debris-flow alert feed](https://data.gov.tw/dataset/148402) every `ALERT_POLL_SECONDS` (600). An alert's `DebrisNo` is the same stream number as in the zone file, so it maps to its roads with no fuzzy matching. Alerts expire after `ALERT_TTL_MINUTES` (180), so a failing feed cannot keep a road closed forever, and a malformed response is treated as an error, never as "no alerts". `road_closures` is a view over the alerts and `road_hazards`; `GET /api/alerts` lists them and the map colours alerted zones red or yellow.
- **Asking in the chat.** 「現在有土石流警戒嗎」 is answered by code from the same alerts (`services/chat/alert_answer.py`): each alert's level, village, stream number and the road length closed or to avoid, narrowed to a county or township if the question names one, with the source and the time of the last update in Taiwan time. "No alerts" is only said when the feed was updated successfully within `ALERT_TTL_MINUTES`; otherwise expired alerts would look the same as no alerts, so the answer says the state cannot be confirmed and points to the agency's website. 「黃色警戒是什麼意思」 still gets the knowledge passage.
- **People inside a red zone can still leave.** The road under a starting point is never closed: those are exactly the people who have to get out.
- **No safe road means no route.** A pair with no path is retried on the whole network (0.14 s); if there is still none, the shelter is not used (`unreachable`), and a township with no safe road to any shelter is listed in `cut_off`, its people counted as unplaced. There is no straight-line fallback here, because the straight line would cross the closed zone.
- **What is reported.** Rankings use the weighted cost; answers report the route's real length, its walking and driving time, and `hazard_m`, the metres that still cross a zone when there is no safer road.
- **Measured.** In a 10 km flood simulation by car, 太麻里's 26 routes cross 6.38 km of zones without the weighting and 4.79 km with it, for 1.8 km more driving; 臺東市's 0.21 km does not change because there is no other road. (With township centroids as origins the figures were 6.18 → 2.21 km and 0.12 km.) A red alert on 宜縣DF096/097/115/116 closes the Su-Hua Highway between 南澳 and 東澳. With township centroids as origins (until 2026-11-07) this made the 東澳 shelters unreachable and placed 437 people instead of 544; with village origins 東澳里 and 東岳村 lie north of the closure and fill their own shelters, so the same alert no longer changes the plan (506 placed either way). Putting every debris-flow zone within 10 km of 頭城鎮 on red cuts off 合興里, 大溪里 and 龜山里 on the coast road and two shelters, and 2,855 people are placed instead of 3,015; around 太麻里鄉 it cuts off 大王村 and three shelters (1,070 instead of 1,300).
- **Not yet checked against a real alert.** On 2026-10-31 the feed reported no alerts, so the `DebrisNo` format is inferred from the dataset description and the zone file.

## Travel time: walking or driving

- **Cost is time.** Walking is 1.0 m/s on every road, the speed Japanese tsunami-evacuation planning uses to allow for older people and children; with one speed everywhere, the fastest walk is also the shortest, so walking (the default) ranks exactly as road distance did. Driving uses a speed per OSM road class in `models/travel.py`, for example 50 km/h on provincial roads, 20 km/h in lanes and 10 km/h on farm tracks. The legs to and from the road are always walked.
- **Both times are reported.** Nearest-shelter results and every flow carry `walk_min` and `drive_min` for the chosen route, and the route tooltip shows them.
- **Choosing.** `mode` (`walk` / `drive`) on `/api/simulate_disaster` and `/api/nearest_shelter`; the simulation panel has a travel-mode selector.
- **One-way streets apply to driving only.** The network file keeps OSM's `oneway` tag (motorways and roundabouts count as one-way even without it; `oneway=-1` ways are flipped). Driving routes on a directed graph where the reverse direction of a one-way edge is closed; walking ignores it. Most one-way edges are provincial roads drawn as two separate carriageways, so before this a driving route could run up the opposite carriageway. Measured on five scenarios (1,048 township–shelter pairs): 47.5% of driving times changed, by a median of +0.5% (p90 +3.5%, at most +64%); no shelter became unreachable, and queries still take 0.03–0.09 s.
- **Measured.** In a 60 km scenario across the east coast, driving times cut the average drive per person from 20.0 to 16.8 minutes for 2 % more distance. From the centre of Hualien City, driving ranks 花蓮市公所 (0.89 km) second, ahead of 國立花蓮農業職業學校 (0.56 km), because its road is faster.

## Road bottlenecks when driving

The assignment only limits shelter capacity; roads are treated as if they could carry any number of cars. When the mode is `drive`, the plan also says which roads would clog and how long they take to clear, without changing who goes where (`services/simulation/bottlenecks.py`).

- **How.** Each route used by the plan comes back from `pgr_withPoints` as the edges it traverses, in order and with direction. The people on every route are added up per edge and direction, divided by 2.5 people per car, and divided by that edge's capacity per hour; an edge's clearance time is vehicles ÷ capacity. Edges of the same road (by route number, otherwise by name) carrying the same traffic are merged into one stretch, named after its longest edge, with the bridges it crosses listed. Each road appears once in the top ten, at its slowest stretch.
- **Route numbers in OpenStreetMap are incomplete.** Parts of a numbered road often carry only the street name: 宜6 林尾路 continues west as plain 林尾路, so the same road was listed twice, once with its number and once without. An edge without a number now takes the number of a connected edge of the same name, and passes it along the chain; requiring the edges to touch keeps roads of the same name in other townships (中山路一段) apart. A stretch shared by two routes (`7;宜18`) belongs to both: listing it counts for 台7 and 宜18, and it is skipped only when both are already listed. Skipping it when either was listed hid real bottlenecks: in the 玉里 flood, 台30 玉長隧道 comes first and the shared 台9/台30 花東縱谷公路, 14 minutes and the slowest stretch of 台9, disappeared. Over the 80 driving scenarios the top tens had 12 pairs of the same street with and without a number, now none, and 29 pairs sharing a route number, now 14, each a shared stretch that is the slowest of its other route. Different routes on a street of the same name, such as 宜14 and 宜18 on 中山路一段, stay separate. No scenario's slowest stretch or time changed, and one gained its number (富里鄉 earthquake, 台23 中山路).
- **Capacity** comes from the 2011 Taiwan Highway Capacity Manual (Institute of Transportation), per direction: motorways 2,000 passenger cars per hour per lane (the outer-lane figure); roads with two or more lanes each way 1,700–2,100 per lane by free-flow speed (table 11.6); one lane each way 1,450, half the manual's two-way 2,900 for two-lane highways, which the manual itself says still needs field data. Lanes, alleys and farm roads are not in the manual: they are assumed to carry half that, 725, the least supported number here. Only 5% of the road length has a lane count in OpenStreetMap, so missing counts default by road class.
- **Route ends are left out.** Each village starts from a single point and each shelter is a single door, so the few hundred metres around them would always come out as the worst bottleneck. Edges whose midpoint lies within 500 m of either end of the route are not counted. Over 80 scenarios (41 townships × flood and earthquake, driving), excluding 1,000 m left 80% of the people uncounted, 500 m 60% and 250 m 42%; at 250 m local streets such as 公正路 in Yilan City began to rank near the top, which is the artefact the rule removes. The slowest stretch was the same in all three settings: 台9 中正路一段 in Yilan City in the earthquake scenario, 0.66, 0.69 and 0.73 hours.
- **What is reported.** `evacuation.bottlenecks` in the `/api/simulate_disaster` response (`null` when walking): `top` (label, route number, name, road class, `bridge` and the named `bridges`, people, vehicles, `capacity_vph`, `hours`, length, and a point on it for the map), `max_hours`, `vehicles`, `local_people` (routes too short to cross any counted edge) and `uncounted_people` (no road route, so placed by straight-line distance). It adds 0.01–0.06 s to a simulation. On the map each listed road is a purple ring at its slowest stretch, larger the longer it takes to clear, with the cars, capacity and time in its tooltip; when the animation ends the chat says which stretch is slowest, the next three, how many people were too close to count, and that the figure is a lower bound (`static/js/crowd.js`). The [capacity gap report](#capacity-gap-report) lists the slowest stretch of every scenario.
- **What it found.** With every evacuee leaving at once, no stretch of road in the 80 scenarios needs more than 0.7 hours to clear, and only one needs more than half an hour. Capacity in the shelters, not on the roads, is the constraint. Capacity and occupancy only scale the hours: 30% less capacity makes every stretch 43% slower without changing the order.
- **Limits.** A lower bound, not a forecast: everyone leaves at once, queues do not spill back upstream, and traffic is all passenger cars, with no scooters or heavy vehicles. Signalised junctions are not modelled, and in towns they, rather than road sections, are usually the bottleneck; together with the 60% of people on short local routes, this means town centres are where the figure says least. Walking is not computed: the largest village (吉安鄉慶豐村, 7,835 people) on a 2 m footway clears in under an hour at the manual's 72–80 people per metre per minute (table 19.5). The assignment does not yet react to congestion.

## Capacity gap report

[reports/capacity_gap.md](../reports/capacity_gap.md) answers the planning question behind the simulation: which townships cannot house their own people, by how much, and which villages a red alert cuts off. The same report is a page on the site at `/report` (linked from the navbar as 容量報告), with tables you can sort and filter by county. It is regenerated with one command and does not touch the database's occupancy:

```bash
docker exec disaster_app python tools/report/capacity_report.py
```

- **Part 1, each township's own capacity, uses no assumptions.** Registered capacity as a share of the township's population, counted separately for earthquakes (only shelters the Fire Agency lists for earthquakes) and floods (only shelters listed for floods and outside debris-flow zones). Compare it with the evacuation ratio and a township office can see at a glance whether it can house its own residents.
- **Part 2, scenarios, uses the same code as the map.** Each of the 41 townships is the centre of a 10 km earthquake, flood and flood-with-red-alert scenario (every debris-flow stream in the township on red, roads inside closed). The destinations come from `services/simulation/scenario.py`, which `/api/simulate_disaster` also uses, and the assignment is the same min-cost flow in pgRouting. Shelters are treated as empty, so a simulation left on the map does not change the report.
- **The evacuation ratio is an assumption, so every unplaced count is a range.** The table gives the count at the ratio used by the simulation (12% earthquake, 22% flood) and at ×0.8 and ×1.2. It also gives the ceiling: the share of the affected population the shelters in the circle could take if everyone evacuated, which no ratio changes.
- **Road bottlenecks when driving.** The earthquake and flood scenarios are run once more by car at the base ratio, with their own assignment and routes, as when the map is set to car, and each records its three slowest roads ([how they are estimated](#road-bottlenecks-when-driving)). The report lists the slowest stretch of each scenario with its cars, capacity, time to clear and the people too close to count; the page sorts by any column and filters by county. The worst is 台9 中正路一段 in the Yilan City earthquake, about 1,000 cars and 41 minutes; 2 of 79 scenarios have a stretch that takes half an hour or more. The figures are lower bounds, and next to the shortfall in shelter places the roads are not the main constraint.
- **One source for the findings.** The highlights, the list of data to check with the township office, the cut-off villages and the road bottlenecks are computed once in `services/simulation/capacity_report.py`; the Markdown file and the page both use them. The page reads the JSON the script wrote and never runs scenarios itself.
- **Same data, same report.** Shelters in a circle come back sorted by name. Without an order they came in the table's physical order, which changes whenever a simulation writes occupancy, and the min-cost flow breaks ties by that order, so a rerun after using the map moved a few dozen people between shelters and changed the driving figures. Two reports with a full browser test in between are now identical.
- **The red-alert scenario writes temporary alerts.** Alerts with source `capacity_report` are written to `hazard_alerts` for the run, expire after 10 minutes, and are deleted when it finishes. While it runs (about 70 s for 116 scenarios, half of it the driving runs), routes on the site avoid those zones too.
- **What it found** (2026-11-14): registered capacity in the three counties is 70,803, 7.3% of the 965,887 registered residents. No shelter in 卓溪鄉, 玉里鎮 or 萬榮鄉 (32,810 residents) is listed for earthquakes, which is probably incomplete data but sends everyone in an earthquake to the neighbouring townships. 臺東市 has 102,065 residents and 705 registered places. A red alert on every debris-flow stream of a township leaves 18 villages in 12 scenarios with no safe road to any shelter.
- **Limitations.** Registered population, a fixed evacuation ratio, one origin per village, registered capacity, a red alert on a whole township at once, which is harsher than most real alerts, and road bottlenecks that are lower bounds. The report is written in Chinese only, because its readers are township and county offices.

## Spatial Index

Every distance query casts `geom` to `geography` to work in metres, but the original GiST index was built on `geom`, so PostgreSQL never used it and scanned the whole table. An index has to be built on the same expression the query uses, so `idx_shelters_geog` indexes `(geom::geography)`. `ensure_schema` creates it at startup, so existing databases get it too. `EXPLAIN` now shows an Index Scan; the hazard-zone table gets the same kind of index.
