# Heavy rain and flooding (English translation, draft)

## heavy-rain-levels｜Heavy rain levels: heavy, extremely heavy, torrential, extremely torrential
- reviewed: no

When heavy rain is expected, the Central Weather Administration issues a heavy rain advisory (大雨或豪雨特報). The levels are:
Heavy rain (大雨): 80 mm or more in 24 hours, or 40 mm or more in one hour.
Extremely heavy rain (豪雨): 200 mm or more in 24 hours, or 100 mm or more in 3 hours.
Torrential rain (大豪雨): 350 mm or more in 24 hours, or 200 mm or more in 3 hours.
Extremely torrential rain (超大豪雨): 500 mm or more in 24 hours.
Intense rain often causes landslides, rockfalls and debris flows in the mountains; low-lying areas should guard against flooding, and areas near rivers should watch for sudden rises in river levels and flash floods.

## flood-alert｜What flood alert levels 1 and 2 mean
- reviewed: no

Flood alerts are issued by the Water Resources Agency (經濟部水利署) based on real-time rainfall in each township, at two levels:
Level 2 alert: rain keeps accumulating; if it does not stop, flood-prone villages and road sections may start to flood within the next 3 hours.
Level 1 alert: rainfall has reached the alert threshold; flood-prone villages and road sections may already be flooding, or may flood at any time within the next hour.

## flood-before｜Living in a low-lying area: what to prepare before a flood
- reviewed: no

Move important documents, electrical appliances and clothes upstairs (the second floor or higher) or somewhere high, and put important belongings on high shelves.
Move cars and scooters to a safe place as early as possible.
Check that roof drainpipes and gutters are not blocked, clear leaves and rubbish from drains, and check the roof and outer walls for cracks.
Have sandbags ready or install flood barriers.
When a typhoon or low-pressure system approaches, the sea is higher than usual, and if this coincides with high tide, waves may surge ashore and flood the land with seawater; coastal residents should be especially alert.

## flood-during｜How to shelter during a flood
- reviewed: no

In a flood, the general rule is to shelter where you are or move up (vertical evacuation, to a higher floor of your home); people living in low-lying areas should go to a shelter as instructed.
If you live in a low-lying, flood-prone area, keep watching the rivers and drains, and if disaster strikes, follow the rescue workers' instructions to evacuate.
Do not go into flooded basements, to avoid electric shock or drowning; at underpasses, check how deep the water is and do not force your way through.
When evacuating, follow the routes the government gives, do not take shortcuts, and never risk crossing a river.
