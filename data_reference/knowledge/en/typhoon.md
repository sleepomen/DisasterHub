# Typhoons (English translation, draft)

## typhoon-warning｜Typhoon warnings: sea warnings and land warnings
- reviewed: no

There are two kinds of typhoon warning, the sea typhoon warning and the land typhoon warning. Both are issued in advance, before the typhoon's force-7 storm circle may reach an area, so when a warning is issued there may not be any wind or rain locally yet.
Sea typhoon warning: issued 24 hours before the force-7 storm circle is forecast to reach the waters within 100 km of Taiwan, Penghu, Kinmen or Matsu.
Land typhoon warning: issued 18 hours before the force-7 storm circle is forecast to reach land on Taiwan, Penghu, Kinmen or Matsu.
After either warning is issued, it is updated every 3 hours, with extra updates when needed. When a sea warning is in force and the storm circle may reach land on Green Island (綠島) or Orchid Island (蘭嶼), both islands are added to the warning area.

## typhoon-warning-lifted｜When typhoon warnings are lifted
- reviewed: no

The land typhoon warning is lifted when the typhoon's force-7 storm circle leaves land on Taiwan, Penghu, Kinmen and Matsu; the sea typhoon warning is lifted when the storm circle leaves the waters near Taiwan, Kinmen and Matsu. If the typhoon turns away or dissipates, the warnings can be lifted directly.
Because the two warnings are lifted under different conditions, the sea warning may still be in force after the land warning has been lifted.

## typhoon-intensity｜Typhoon intensity: mild, moderate and strong
- reviewed: no

The Central Weather Administration grades typhoons into three levels by the maximum sustained wind speed near the centre:
Mild typhoon (輕度颱風): 17.2–32.6 metres per second (62–117 km/h), Beaufort force 8–11.
Moderate typhoon (中度颱風): 32.7–50.9 metres per second (118–183 km/h), force 12–15.
Strong typhoon (強烈颱風): 51.0 metres per second or more (184 km/h or more), force 16 or above.
A typhoon makes landfall when its centre moves from the sea onto land. The strongest wind and rain are around the centre, so serious damage often occurs near the landfall point.

## typhoon-before-home｜Before a typhoon: what to prepare at home
- reviewed: no

Keep track of the typhoon through TV, radio, the internet or the 166 and 167 recorded weather lines.
Close all doors and windows that are not needed, and board them up if necessary. Clear drains and ditches so they do not block and flood.
Take down or secure anything hanging outside or in the yard (signboards, balcony flower pots) so it cannot be blown down and hurt someone; brace garden trees and prune their branches.
Move things that must not get wet to a suitable place.
Keep three to four days of food, drinking water and dry rations; prepare candles, a torch, batteries and phone batteries in case of a power cut. Check the wiring and watch any open flame to prevent fire.

## typhoon-before-evacuate｜Before a typhoon: who should leave early
- reviewed: no

If you live on a hillside or where earth and rock fall easily: follow the government's precautionary evacuation (預防性疏散撤離) and move to a safe area early; if you have no relatives or friends to stay with, you can go to an emergency shelter.
If you live in a low-lying area that may flood, or next to a river: move to higher ground early.
If you live on the coast: watch the tides and guard against seawater flooding inland.
If your house is made of bamboo or earth blocks: it is safer to move to a safe place for the time being.
If you are hiking or camping, go home as early as possible and tell your family your route and plans; if you have not set out yet, cancel the trip.
Do not go to the riverside to harvest crops or work, and do not go to the coast or to streams to watch the waves, play in the water, collect stones, fish or angle.

## typhoon-during｜During a typhoon: what to watch out for
- reviewed: no

If your home is solid and there is no special need, stay inside and do not go out. If the wind suddenly drops, the eye of the typhoon may be passing; do not rush outside.
Follow the news about reservoir releases, damage and road closures. If you live in a low-lying area prone to flooding, watch the rivers and drains, and if disaster strikes, follow the rescue workers' instructions to evacuate.
Do not go into flooded basements, to avoid electric shock or drowning; at underpasses, check how deep the water is and do not force your way through.
If you are driving, slow down and do not enter closed areas; in strong wind, stop at the roadside or in a safe place until it passes.
In a power cut, use a torch first; keep candles away from anything flammable and stand them securely. Do not touch hanging or fallen power lines; tell the power company or call 1999.
Do not go to the sea to fish, watch the tide or play in the water; outdoors, do not shelter under trees.

## typhoon-evacuation｜During a typhoon: how to evacuate and shelter
- reviewed: no

Around each village and along main roads there are disaster evacuation signboards (防災避難看板) showing the name and direction of the shelters and how many people they can take. In floods the general rule is to shelter where you are or move to an upper floor (vertical evacuation); people in low-lying areas should go to a shelter. Evacuation is usually guided by the village chief, the police or firefighters.
Beforehand: know the disasters your community may face and its evacuation and shelter plan, learn where the shelters are and alternative routes, prepare an emergency go-bag and agree on a meeting point; if you have pets, check in advance whether they can be kept somewhere.
When evacuating: follow government instructions and keep listening to the radio; leave early and tell your family where you are going and when you will arrive; lock the doors and windows before you leave; wear sturdy shoes, long sleeves and long trousers, and a helmet; look out for your neighbours and share rides; follow the routes the government gives, do not take shortcuts, watch for debris flows and broken bridges, and never risk crossing a river.

## typhoon-after｜After a typhoon: what to watch out for
- reviewed: no

Only go out once you are sure the typhoon has moved away. Outside, watch for falling objects; if you see fallen objects, flooding or blocked roads, call 110 or 119.
Drains and holes covered by water cannot be seen; do not force your way through. Never touch fallen power lines with your hands; tell the power company to repair them.
Keep calls to say you are safe short, so the lines are not jammed.
Boil tap water, well water and natural water before drinking it. Empty standing water from vases, water jars, tyres, plant-pot saucers and the like so mosquitoes cannot breed; keep perishable rubbish separate and follow the environmental agency's collection.
Infectious diseases spread easily after a typhoon: clean up and remove dirt as soon as possible, and see a doctor at once if you find signs of infection.
